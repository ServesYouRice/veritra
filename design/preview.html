Veritra — five ways the app could look

The first row is what ships today. Below it are eleven variants of one direction — A's structure on C's plum, with the Viber-adjacent indigo and the gradient removed. E–J are six accent hues. K1–K5 spend no accent hue at all, varying only the temperature of the near-white that carries the accent slots. Ground, type, radii and density are identical throughout, so every comparison here is a pure colour comparison. The four original directions follow at the end for reference. Every screen is drawn from one shared markup template and re-skinned by CSS variables, so the differences you see are genuinely only palette, type, shape and density — the layout improvements are common to all of them. Flip light/dark with the button above; each direction is tuned for both. Pick a direction, then read design/directions.md.

E · Ember

Revision of A + C

Ink's structure on Aurora's plum. Surfaces separate by tone, not shadow, over 1px hairlines; 12/20 radii, 62px rows. The indigo is gone — it sat about ten degrees from Viber's #7360F2 — and so is the gradient, the coloured shadow and the 28px radii that made Aurora feel overdone. The accent is a single flat ember, held to four places: send button, active nav, unread badge, focus ring. Sent bubbles stay on tone, because a column of amber would just be loudness in a different hue. Best if you want warm and editorial, and are willing to reshoot the brand assets.

Warm. Full break from the Indigo/Sky brand — docs/branding/ would need redoing.

F · Dusk

Revision of A + C

Identical structure and plum ground to E — same tone-only depth, same hairlines, same radii and density — but the accent stays Sky. That is the half of the existing brand that was never the problem: only the Viber-adjacent indigo is dropped, so the mark and wordmark need a ground swap rather than a new palette. Flat, no gradient. Sent bubbles do carry the accent here, since sky-blue is the conventional read and tests at 7.8:1. Best if you want the plum without paying for a rebrand.

Cool. Keeps the Sky half of docs/branding/; only the navy ground changes to plum.

G · Rose

Plum family

The closest accent to the ground itself — rose sits about 20 degrees from plum, so the whole screen reads as one temperature. Warmest and friendliest of the set; this is the approachable feeling C was reaching for, without the gradient. Watch for pink-on-plum drifting toward a consumer-social read.

Rose #F2879F Rose lt #B03A55 Plum #16111F

H · Fuchsia

Plum family

Rose pushed to full saturation — the boldest option here, and the only one that competes with the ground for attention. Sent bubbles stay on tone; tiled fuchsia would be exactly the loudness that got C rejected. Watch for magenta-on-purple reading gaudy if it ever leaks past the four accent slots.

Fuchsia #EE7BD0 Fuchsia lt #A83A8C Plum #16111F

I · Jade

Complementary

Plum's near-opposite, so the accent separates hardest from the ground — 10:1, the highest in the set. Cool, clean, slightly clinical. One real cost: green is the natural colour for “verified” and “encrypted”. Spend it as the brand accent and the security states need a different signal.

Jade #42D6A0 Jade lt #0F7A57 Plum #16111F

J · Azure

Complementary

A true blue rather than F's cyan-leaning sky — deeper, more classical, the most conventional “messenger” read of the seven. Note it is the closest of this set to the hue that was rejected: 46 degrees from Viber's #7360F2, against 89 for Rose and 100 for Jade. Still unmistakably blue, not violet, but it is the one to check against the thing you did not like.

Azure #6FB2FF Azure lt #1667C7 Plum #16111F

K · Bone — five temperatures

No accent hue

No accent colour at all. A near-white carries every accent slot, so the plum ground is the entire identity and nothing competes with it. The five below differ only in the temperature of that near-white — and in the muted grey biased to match it, since a neutral that leans toward its own bone reads as chosen rather than inherited. Ground, type, radii and density are identical, so this is a pure temperature comparison. In light mode each one inverts: the bone becomes the paper, the accent becomes a deep tone of the same temperature.

The real payoff is below — with no brand hue spent, the whole colour wheel stays free for meaning. These four states are unambiguous here in a way they cannot be in any direction whose brand already owns green or amber:

Verified — safety number matches Warning — key expiring Error — device revoked Info — syncing

Dark #4ADE80 · #FBBF24 · #FB7185 · #7DD3FC — 6.9:1 to 11.1:1 on plum. Light #15803D · #B45309 · #BE123C · #0369A1 — 4.56:1 to 5.7:1 on paper, so the light set only just clears AA and should not be shrunk below body size.

K1 · Chalk

Neutral

Faintly cool, almost pure. The starkest of the five and the most contemporary — this is the white-pill-on-dark look. Least warmth, most edge.

Chalk #F4F4F6 Deep #332F3A Muted #A39FA6

K2 · Bone

The original

Warm paper — the version you saw first. Enough warmth to feel like a document rather than a dashboard, without reading as sepia. The middle of the range, and the safest pick if you do not want to commit hard either way.

Bone #EDE4DA Deep #3A2E42 Muted #A89EA6

K3 · Greige

Warmest

Deeper and warmer — the accent stops being "white" and starts being a material. The most analog of the five; its light mode is the closest thing here to actual paper stock. Watch for the warmth reading as dated if the type is not kept crisp.

Greige #DCD3C6 Deep #403528 Muted #A79C93

K4 · Ash

Most cohesive

The bone leans into the plum instead of away from it — the accent is a pale tint of the ground itself. The most cohesive of the five, and the one that most looks like a single considered colour rather than a dark screen with white on it. Cost: least contrast in character; it can read soft.

Ash #E4DDEA Deep #372C43 Muted #A79EB4

K5 · Steel

Coolest

Leans blue-grey, against the plum's warmth. The most technical read of the five — the slight cool cast reads as instrument rather than document, which suits a security product. It is also the sharpest contrast of temperature against the ground.

Steel #D8E0E8 Deep #2C333B Muted #9FA8B4

A · Ink

Recommended

Dark-first editorial. Surfaces separate by tone, not shadow, over 1px hairlines. The Indigo→Sky brand gradient is rationed to exactly four places: send button, active nav, unread badge, focus ring. Tight type, 27px screen titles, uppercase micro-labels. Reads like Linear, Vercel. Best if you want Veritra to feel precise and serious — and it is the only direction that already matches docs/branding/concept-06.

Canvas #0B1220 Surface #151D31 Raised #1C2740 Indigo #6366F1 Sky #0EA5E9 Text #EEF1FA

B · Vault

Greyscale everywhere except one signal green, which is spent only on encryption and verification state. Encryption becomes the single coloured thing in the product, so the security story is the visual story. Squarer corners, heavier type, borders you can see. Reads like Proton, Tailscale, 1Password. Best if you want trust through restraint.

Paper #FAFAFA Near-black #08090A Border #D6D6D6 Signal #22C55E Signal dk #16A34A

C · Aurora

Light-first, with a deep-plum dark mode. Surfaces float on soft indigo-tinted shadows, a gradient glow sits behind the brand moment, and sent bubbles carry the gradient itself. Big radii, generous spacing. Reads like Bluesky, Beeper, iOS 26. Best if you want Veritra to feel approachable to non-technical people.

Canvas #F7F7FB Plum #16111F Indigo #6366F1 Sky #38BDF8 Text #1A1630

D · Terminal

Dense and technical. Compact rows fit ~40% more per screen, and every identifier — account IDs, invite codes, verification codes, fingerprints — is monospace by default. This app is full of them; invite_screen.dart already reaches for monospace on its own. Reads like Warp, Ghostty, Datadog. Best if your users are the people running the server.
