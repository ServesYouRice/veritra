# UI audit (2026-11-14)

Every screen of the app rendered, measured and compared with the platform
rules and published research. Result: packages U1–U38 in
[`STATE.md`](../../STATE.md#ui-audit-2026-11-14). Nothing in `mobile/lib/`
changed. Before and after for 72 screens (owner's private page):
<https://claude.ai/artifact/GLBQ9gpKAJHvbAqq9FRspC>.

## Method

- A temporary widget-test harness pumped the real screens with fake API and
  crypto fixtures and realistic content, at 360 × 800 dp, in dark and light,
  with real fonts (Roboto, Material Icons) and real shadows. 525 states, each
  as a PNG and a JSON of measurements. Text scale 2.0 for the chat list, a
  thread, the connect screen and all of Settings.
- Per state the harness recorded every text run (size, weight, colour,
  painted background, contrast), every tappable semantics node (size, label)
  and Flutter's `androidTapTargetGuideline` and `labeledTapTargetGuideline`.
- The harness is not in the repo (it wrote to a session folder and has no
  place in `scripts/test`); U38 is the package that would add one properly.

## App-wide findings

1. Small print: second lines at 11.5 and labels at 11 (U10).
2. "Encrypted" on every chat row, chat header and bubble, where the last message would go (U12, U13).
3. Own and others' bubbles differ by one tonal step (U14).
4. Bottom tabs hide their labels under 440 dp; many icon-only actions (U11, U15, U16).
5. Settings is one list of 31 rows (U18).
6. Nouns written for engineers (U19; list below).
7. Destructive actions behind bare icons, some with no confirmation or undo (U15, U16).
8. `errorContainer` used for routine states, so real danger does not stand out (U17).
9. Five to ten lines of explanation inside rows (U18, U30).
10. One row collapses at large text (U2).

## Sources

Read in the original: WCAG 2.2 (1.4.1, 1.4.3, 1.4.11, 2.5.5, 2.5.8); Apple
Human Interface Guidelines (typography, buttons, tab bars, accessibility);
Material 3 type scale and component touch-target defaults; Appt.org font-size
statistics (about 26% of phone users enlarge text).

Confirmed from published abstracts and summaries only (publisher sites were
not reachable): Legge & Bigelow 2011 (critical print size); Rello, Pielot &
Marcos 2016 (font size and readability); Parhi, Karlson & Bederson 2006 (thumb
target size); Hick 1952 and Hyman 1953; Treisman & Gelade 1980 and Duncan &
Humphreys 1989 (visual search); Abu-Salma et al. 2017 (users do not
understand end-to-end encryption); Vaziripour et al. 2017 and 2018
(authentication ceremony); Dechand et al. 2016 (key-fingerprint formats);
Felt et al. 2015 (opinionated warning design); Anderson et al. 2015 (warning
habituation); Piepenbrock et al. 2013 (display polarity); Hoober 2013
(one-handed use); Wroblewski 2009 (inline validation, n=22); and Nielsen
Norman Group articles on heuristics, icon labels, progressive disclosure,
placeholders, confirmation dialogs, hidden navigation, empty states and plain
language.

## Limits

- Android rendering only (Roboto, Material); iOS was not rendered.
- One phone size, plus one 1280 × 800 and one 1100 × 800 capture.
- Fixture data, not a real account; platform sheets (passkey, pickers,
  keyboard) and live camera or video were not captured.
- The reading-speed threshold is from the literature; converting it to about
  size 13 on a phone at 30 cm is this audit's own arithmetic.
- No user testing was done. Each suggestion's confidence is stated on the
  before-and-after page.
