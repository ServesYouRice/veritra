<goal>
Make the iOS 'New encrypted message' notification appear without depending on background execution, while still carrying no message, sender or conversation data and without duplicate alerts.

This matters because it fixes:
- iOS user notification relies on app execution for a background push (medium)
  The only user-visible notification on iOS is a local notification posted from application(_:didReceiveRemoteNotification:fetchCompletionHandler:). It runs only when iOS grants the app background execution for the push. iOS throttles content-available (background) pushes, may delay or drop them under Low Power Mode or budget limits, and never launches an app the user has force-quit. Under those conditions no notification appears at all. If the server instead sends visible alert pushes, this handler posts a second local notification on top of the system one.
</goal>

If so, how should already-installed app versions be handled? Their didReceiveRemoteNotification still posts a local notification when not active, so they would show the system alert and a duplicate local one. Options are to accept transient duplicates, to send alerts only to subscriptions that advertise a new capability at registration (an API change to POST /api/v1/push/subscriptions), or to gate by a minimum client version. Apple's 'Pushing background updates to your App' documentation states that background notifications are low priority, are not guaranteed, and do not launch a user-terminated app. A visible alert is the documented way to show text without app execution, which supports moving to alerts. No option for the old-client duplicate is known to work without either accepting duplicates or changing the registration API.
</open_questions>
